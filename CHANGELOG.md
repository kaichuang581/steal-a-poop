# Changelog

All user-visible changes, newest first.

## 0.8.1

- The six animals are rebuilt to match Brian's six-view sheets one to one: sheep, pig, cow, goat, horse and chicken.
- Every animal is drawn at its lineup size (sheep 1, chicken 0.66, pig 0.86, cow 1.14, goat 1.15, horse 1.54), scaled evenly so its proportions never change, on the farm too.
- The horse's neck is one flat leaning block with a stepped mane, and its snout sits flush with the bottom of its head.
- A frozen sheep in its thickest coat is fully inside its ice, and its tail stays on at every coat level.
- The sheep is rebuilt cell for cell from Brian's sheet: a small face set into its wool, one-cell eyes, small ears, a cloud of a body on short legs.
- Scenery colours are flat: no speckle, no grain, plain snow. Pines are tiered with snow on each tier. The air is clearer and Home is no longer blurred.
- No footprints, sled ruts or worn tracks pressed into the ground.
- The forest behind the farm is tiered pines too, and the barn has a rounded roof under one blanket of snow.
- Home's two close shots of the sheep stand further back.

## 0.7.1

- Ranked play. Press Play and two cards come up: Casual and Ranked. A ranked game is people only (2 to 4, no farm sheep) on the standard rules; it starts by itself a few seconds after a second player joins the queue, and leaving one counts as a loss. Finishing ahead of other players earns points and finishing behind loses them, more when the other player was ranked above you. There are eight ranks, from Lamb to Golden Fleece, the first seven in divisions I, II and III of 100 points each; everyone starts at Lamb I, and losses are softened in the bottom three ranks. Your rank is on your profile and on the results of every ranked game. Ranked needs an account.
- Quick play and Play are always games with real people. The farm sheep (bots) are in the lobbies you create, Practice and the Tutorial.
- A shorter menu. Custom game is gone as a button of its own: Create lobby now does both. In your lobby you add as many farm sheep (bots) as you like, from none to seven, and pick how good they are, so a table can be bots only, people only, or both. It opens the way you left your last one.
- Your profile is the box with your name in the top corner. It is no longer a separate item on the menu.
- New leaderboards, up in the sky. The trophy button on the start screen opens a whole screen: a clear winter sky over a sea of cloud, where the top three lounge on tall gold, silver and bronze pedestals, each on its own floating island. First leans back with the trophy, second sits on the edge kicking its heels, and third is fast asleep on its belly with its legs dangling. Their names hang on their pedestals. A wooden beam across the top has the three boards (Ranked, Wins, Level), and a plank board beside the sky lists everyone else on dark stitched wool, with where you stand and how far you are from the podium pinned at its foot. Open places say so. You do not need to be signed in to read them
- The friends list is gone: friend codes, friend requests, the online dots and room invites, for accounts and for guests. A lobby is joined with its party code or its link, as before. The Friends button on the start screen and "Invite a friend" in the lobby went with it
- A new lobby screen, built like the Trading Post and your profile. A snow-topped beam across the top has the room's name and the party code with a button that copies the invite link, and a string of lights hangs under it. Along the bottom are three wooden boards: Players (a line for everyone, with buttons to add or take out bots; every farm sheep wears a BOT tag), Game (how long, how good the bots are, who can join, the rules on one clear line with a "Change rules" button, and the green "Open the gate" sign) and Chat, where each speaker's name is a coloured tag beside what they said. The animals in the pen wear the same name tags as at the table.
- A bigger pen in the lobby, and the animals in it move. It stands in a snowy mountain meadow at night: a three-rail fence with a shut gate, a hay rack, a water trough, straw underfoot and tufts of grass, with pines, boulders, a wooded ridge, mountains and the moon behind. Everyone's animal, farm sheep included, wanders about the pen and stops to graze.
- The game after the tutorial is a real one: its last card sends you into the Quick play queue.
- No more traps. Ending a word on X, Z, Q or J used to win you a wool ball if the next sheep ran out of time. Now nobody ever gains wool: you start with yours and can only lose it. The tutorial, How to play and the tips no longer mention traps, and the letter board no longer turns to ice for those letters.
- A new tutorial. It is one guided round at the barn table with the farm's sheepdog: you play a word, watch the next sheep answer it, read what the bomb and the wool balls are, and play once more. The table stays in view the whole time (the old tutorial blacked it out), and each card points at the real thing it is about: the letter board, the word bar, the bomb, your name.
- The tutorial's hint book only suggests everyday words now. It used to offer words like BEVOR and JOSHER.
- Pressing Enter to play your word in the tutorial no longer also dismisses the next card. The Traps card was being skipped that way.
- Your profile shows your practice bests properly. Instead of one "Practice best" that could have been against any bot, every bot (easy, medium, hard) and starting clock (15, 10, 5 seconds) has its own best on a chalk table. Each run is rated: its words, times 1.5 for the medium bot or a 10 second clock, times 2 for the hard bot or a 5 second clock. Your best-rated run is your Practice rating, so 12 words against the hard bot from 10 seconds (36) counts for more than 31 against the easy bot from 15.
- The profile is a room like the Trading Post: your card pinned to the wall, your standing chalked on a slate, your numbers on six slips of paper. A long longest word no longer breaks onto two lines.
- Cleaner cards everywhere. The paper notes are plain cards now: no pins, no tilt, no torn edges. That covers the missions (which also lose their cork board), your profile, the names over a practice run, the messages over the input, the tutorial's word ideas and the stall's price tags.
- The on/off switches and the volume sliders are ordinary switches and sliders. They were little wooden levers and ropes.
- Going into a game on a slower device, the barn doors no longer come down just as the game is ready and then sit over it. They used to appear at the very moment loading finished and stay up for nearly two more seconds; now the screen clears as soon as the game has drawn.
- The barn doors are on screen the moment the page opens. It used to be a blank page until the game had downloaded, and only then the doors. They are there on a refresh too, which used to show the menu over an empty sky while the farm was built.

## 0.7.0

- Music for every place. The calm lofi now plays on the start screen only, and it is a fuller song: eight chords, a walking bass, a written tune and its answer, and a few bars where the drums drop out. The Trading Post has a shop shuffle, a lobby has its own waiting song, Practice, the Tutorial and Counting Sheep (a lullaby) each have theirs, and a game has "Pass the bomb", which plays harder and faster every round as the fuses get shorter and lets go when the game is over. The sound menu says which song is playing. All of it is made live in the browser: no audio files, no hiss.
- The fuse sounds are real sounds. They were one beep at eight pitches, quieter than the music until the last three seconds. Now the wood block knocks tick and tock, the cowbell clanks, the sleigh bells jingle, the barn owl hoots and is answered, the music box plays a lullaby one note a second, the tin toy chirps and the lullaby rings two soft bells; every second can be heard over the music and the last three are louder, higher and doubled. In the Trading Post a card's speaker shows that it is playing, the music steps back for the sample, and with sound switched off it says so.
- The Trading Post is the inside of a shop, and the only way to get most items is a Feed Sack: 250 Wool Coins for one random item. There are three and they never mix: the Oat Sack (bomb skins), the Corn Sack (bursts) and the Barley Sack (fuse sounds). The odds are chalked on a slate (common 62%, uncommon 28%, rare 8%, legendary 2%); a rare or better is guaranteed within 10 sacks and a legendary within 40; a repeat pays Wool Coins back, and a rare or legendary never repeats. Wool Coins come only from playing. Nothing costs money and nothing changes how a game plays.
- A sack is opened on the fair machine: pull the lever and the reel spins down to what you got. What you get is decided before the reel spins. Now and then the reel comes to rest right beside a rarer item.
- The three sacks stand in crates on a market stall: a striped awning with snow on it, shelves of stock behind, a brass scale, a lantern that moves to the sack you pick, and each sack's name and what you have found on a paper tag.
- Golden Bells, a second currency earned by levelling up, word milestones and Counting Sheep streaks, buy the two items on the Bell Shelf. Missions gives the streak items. The Barn Book shows every item, found or not, where each comes from, and a closer look at any of them: a bomb skin turning on a stand, a burst going off, a fuse sound to listen to.
- Nine of the ten bursts are no longer explosions: when the bomb goes off a haystack, a snow cloud, balloons, a campfire, a clump of heather, an aurora, a little sun, paper lanterns or stars appear where it was. Only Frost still explodes.
- Every bomb skin is its own bomb, with its own shape, its own glow or sparkle, and its own mark left on the floor: Ember is a hot coal that leaves a scorch, Gold leaves coins.
- A beat after a bomb. When a fuse runs out the sheep beside it is knocked dizzy, with stars over its head, and the next sheep's fuse is lit 2.4 seconds later, in solo games and rooms alike. Before, the next clock was already running while the camera was still on the bang, so a 5 or 10 second turn felt like 3.
- The end of a game plays out: the last bang, the winner named on a board, then the barn doors, then the results.
- A profile page, from your name plate or the menu: your stats (different words, wins, longest word, practice and Counting Sheep bests, night streak), the top 100 players by XP, where you stand if you are not among them, and the place to change your name.
- Smoother on sharp screens: the barn table now holds 60 frames a second at resolutions where it ran at about 40. Night games are a little brighter.
- Signed in, the server no longer takes a browser's word for everything: a Counting Sheep streak can rise by one night a day, and the first time a browser's wallet is brought into an account it brings at most a week's streak, two rare items, one legendary and 100 Golden Bells.

## 0.4.0

- The Hunt opens on a lobby instead of throwing you straight into a fight: New Pack, Enter Code, Bestiary or back to the farm. Nothing starts until you choose
- Every pack has a five-character code, shown during the fight, on the results card and in what you share. Type a friend's code, or open their link, to fight the very same predators and rules. "Fight this pack again" replays a code and counts which try it is
- Rules teach before they bite: each rule sign says what it means in plain words, with a word that passes and a word that fails. A face-down card warns that a predator brings a secret rule when it's hurt. RULES shows the cards (reading costs time, not a turn)
- As you type, the Hunt tells you if your word would break a rule, isn't a word, or was already used, and the rule's tag turns red. Sending it anyway still breaks the rule
- When a run ends, a screen tells you what got you (the clock, a broken rule, or the predator's strikes) and a word that would have worked, then lets you fight the pack again, take a new pack, or see your results
- The results are one picture made to be screenshotted: your sheep and the predator, predators beaten and damage, your best word, the pack code and which try, with stamps for a new best or walking home
- After beating a boss you can walk home with your win, or hunt on. Leaving mid-run asks first
- The bestiary: six cards, silhouettes until you meet each predator, with how many you've beaten, the highest level and your biggest hit. Flip a card for when you first met it and its signature move
- A save code in Settings carries your records (Hunt bests, the bestiary, your dream streak and more) to another browser: copy it here, paste it there, see what will change, then restore
- The Hunt keeps a log of your last 50 runs on this device (how far you got and what ended each run), so the Hunt can be tuned from real play. It never leaves the device; admin can copy it out by hand
