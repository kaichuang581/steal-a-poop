# One shared rig carries six readable farm animals

Build all six animals on the sheep's existing rig (the same 7 × 10 × 9-voxel wedge head, the same 3 × 2 eyes with one catchlight, the same part list and poses) and spend every species difference on exactly three things: a height-and-neck ladder (hen 15, pig 20, goat 23, sheep 23, cow 27, horse 30 voxels to the crown on the 0.04 m grid), one silhouette signature attached to the head (pig snout disc, cow horns over leaf ears, goat beard, horse mane and blaze, hen comb, sheep fleece), and one colour family per animal with a tail that points a different way on each. This is the pattern the two best-documented cute-animal casts use: Mojang's farm mobs are one box grammar plus one or two tiny attachments each, and Nintendo's Animal Crossing team gives every species a distinct silhouette while keeping every expression identical across species so the cast reads as one family ([Mojang bedrock-samples](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/pig.geo.json); [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=850169)). Cuteness comes from the baby schema, which is one of the most replicated findings in perception research: round face, high forehead, big low-set eyes, small nose and short limbs raise cuteness ratings in humans, cats, dogs, cars and teddy bears alike ([Glocker et al. 2009](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11); [Borgi et al. 2014](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140721100119.htm)), and the existing sheep already hits the numbers (head 43% of height, 40% forehead above the eye row). The craft rules that keep a 20–30-voxel character legible are subtractive: no feature under 2 voxels in its visible dimension unless it is high-contrast, three tones per material with cool shadows and warm highlights and never pure black, chamfered corners instead of stair-stepping, and a black-fill lineup test ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/voxel-art-reducing-the-greebles); [Stardew Modding Wiki](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/How_to_make_pixel_art)). The hardest practical problem is that three of the six animals are pale (cow white, sheep cream, hen buff) and will merge in a warm barn palette unless the cow's white is pushed cool and the hen's buff warm; the second is that the sheep already wears the red collar and bell, so the cow cannot own the bell and must own Holstein patches instead. The build sheet at the end gives every dimension, hex and accessory, reconciled with the values already in `src/skin/voxel/animals.ts`.

## Six species, one grammar: vary height, neck and attachments only

The strongest evidence on how to keep a small animal cast coherent comes from reading the shipped geometry of the most-played voxel farm. Every Mojang quadruped is one box body, one box head overlapping the body's front, and four identical square-section legs pivoted at the hip line; the pig is distinguished by a single **4 × 3 × 1 snout plate**, the cow by two **1 × 3 × 1 horn posts** and a **4 × 6 × 1 udder plate**, the chicken by a **4 × 2 × 2 beak** with a **2 × 2 × 2 wattle**, the horse by a 12-tall neck tilted 30° with a 2 × 2 mane strip, and the goat by a 55°-drooped head with 2 × 7 × 2 horns ([pig.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/pig.geo.json); [cow.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/cow.geo.json); [chicken.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/chicken.geo.json); [horse_v3.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/horse_v3.geo.json); [goat.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/goat.geo.json)). Head width is 67–80% of body width for pig and cow and 60% for the horse; leg thickness never drops below a third of body width; and the species-level silhouette variables are leg height relative to body height (pig 6 on 8, cow 12 on 10, horse 11 on 10) and neck length. The sheep is the thinnest body of all (8 wide) wearing an inflated wool shell, which is exactly why it reads as "sheep" from across a field.

Nintendo's Animal Crossing team states the same principle from the other direction. At CEDEC 2020 they explained that each species gets a distinctive silhouette so players can tell villagers apart at a distance, that designers start from the silhouette and keep it simple because a trait like "fluffy fur" would narrow what the team could build, and that **all 35 species share the same emotional expressions**, which the team credits with making players want to interact with them ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/?p=850169)). Commercial pixel packs operationalise this literally: a 32 × 32 hooved pack ships one animated hooved body with "interchangeable faces" for cow, goat, sheep and pig ([Modular Hooved Creator](https://pixelspriteshop.itch.io/hooved-pack)). Cast-design guides add the rule that bounds the other side: never straying from consistency erases personality, so every character should get **exactly one unique detail** inside the shared system, and a colour cap of two or three main colours per character keeps the lineup from fragmenting ([UPD Cohesion Guide](https://sites.google.com/view/updcohesionguide/basics); [Brookes Eggleston](https://brookes-eggleston-za54.squarespace.com/blog/worldbuilding-101-how-to-design-unique-yet-unified-races-and-factions)).

The project's own code already follows this shape, which is the first thing the engineer should know. `animals.ts` builds every avatar from the sheep's `HEAD_ROWS` wedge, `EYE_BASES`, lid, brow and mouth parts, legs and body ellipsoid, swapping only ears, muzzle, horns, comb, beard, tail and the `LOOK` colour table (`/Users/brianlee/steal-a-poop/src/skin/voxel/animals.ts`). What it does not yet do is vary height, leg length or neck: every animal stands at the sheep's 23 voxels with the sheep's 2 × 2 × 8 legs, so the lineup separates by colour and face attachment alone. The anatomy notes show that neck length and leg length are the two most efficient species variables (pig has no neck and short legs; horse has a neck one head long and legs equal to chest depth; cow's legs are slightly shorter than its torso height; hen is "low-slung" with the neck flowing into the breast) ([Tuts+ pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794); [artnerds horse proportions](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions); [Tuts+ cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050); [Art in Context chicken](https://artincontext.org/how-to-draw-a-chicken/)). Scaling Mojang's inter-animal ratios straight onto a 23-voxel sheep would put the horse at about 36 voxels, outside the brief, so the recommendation is to keep each animal's internal ratios but compress the ladder: **hen 15, pig 20, goat 23, sheep 23, cow 27, horse 30** to the crown, which is 0.6–1.2 m on the 0.04 m grid and lets the black-fill lineup separate by height (horse > cow > goat ≈ sheep > pig > hen) and width (cow, sheep, pig wide; goat, horse narrow) before any colour is applied.

## Three cues and a tail direction keep each species readable at 20–30 voxels

Each species has a small set of identifiers that every drawing tutorial and comparison page mentions first, and those are the ones that survive at this size because they change the outline rather than the interior. The pig is "a barrel with a head and legs", a huge circular head with no neck, a snout that is a flat bridge "capped with a disc", floppy or erect triangular ears, short sturdy legs and a curly tail ([Tuts+ pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794); [The Pink Pigs](https://thepinkpigs.com/pages/seven-pig-breeds-to-know)). The cow is a long rectangle with a triangle neck, a wide blunt muzzle, horns from a narrow oval on top of the head, large leaf-shaped ears held out sideways, an udder under the hips and a thin tail with a tuft reaching to the hock ([Tuts+ cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050); [Tuts+ bovines](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-animals-cows-and-other-bovines--cms-21683)); it is black and white because **Holsteins are 86% of US dairy cows** and cartoons draw cows bicolour almost by rule ([US Dairy](https://www.usdairy.com/news-articles/why-do-people-think-all-dairy-cows-are-black-and-white); [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BicolorCowsSolidColorBulls)). The goat is the species that must be built against the sheep: a healthy goat's short tail "sticks straight up" while a sheep's hangs down, most goats have beards and sheep never do, goat horns "point upwards and slightly back" where sheep horns curl at the sides, and goats are slimmer ([Open Sanctuary](https://opensanctuary.org/spot-the-difference-distinguishing-between-sheep-and-goats/); [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/); [Tuts+ goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948)). The horse is proportion itself: neck one head long, body 2.5–3 heads, legs equal to chest depth, the whole animal fitting a square, with the mane, tail, lower legs and ear rims forming the black "points" of a bay coat ([artnerds](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions); [Wikipedia: Equine coat color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equine_coat_color)). The hen is a red comb and red wattles, a yellow beak, three toes forward and one back, and a low round body; the comb must stay small and may flop to one side, because a large erect comb and sickle tail feathers read as rooster ([poultry.extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/external-anatomy-of-chickens/); [Modern Farmer](https://modernfarmer.com/2021/05/5-differences-between-hens-and-roosters/)). The sheep baseline is a fleece everywhere except the face, with a hanging or docked tail, no beard and usually no horns ([AnimalWised](https://animalwised.com/what-is-the-difference-between-a-goat-and-a-sheep-1364.html)).

Two of those variables are cheap enough in voxels to deserve special attention. Ear set is the first: horse ears are small upright wedges (oversize them and the silhouette slides toward donkey, which Mojang models as the same ear box stretched from 3 to 7 tall and splayed 15°); cow ears are wide flat slabs straight out sideways at eye level below the horns; pig ears are large triangles either upright (Yorkshire) or folded forward (Landrace, the cuter read); goat ears are either erect-forward (Alpine, Saanen) or long pendulous "bell" ears (Nubian, Boer); sheep ears are small and horizontal ([UKY Goat Discovery](https://afs.mgcafe.uky.edu/extension/4-h-youth/livestock/goat-discovery/breeds); [horse_v3.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/horse_v3.geo.json)). Because the sheep already has small horizontal leaf ears and the cow takes the long horizontal leaf, the goat must take the hanging Nubian ear so that ear alone separates goat from sheep and cow; the current code already drops the goat ear to 6 long, and the build sheet keeps that but angles it down. Tail direction is the second: the roster gives each animal a different one for free (pig corkscrew up, goat stub up, hen fan up, cow rod down with tuft, horse hair down, sheep puff down), and these read because a spiral, an up-stub and a down-rod are distinct outline changes rather than interior detail.

Colour is the free third channel on a flat-shaded model, and the legibility ranking follows directly from the breed data. Holstein patches are the single best cue in the set because three to five large irregular dark blobs on white read at any size; flat pink identifies the pig because nothing else in the roster is pink; a bay horse gets three contrasting zones (brown body, near-black mane, tail and socks, white blaze) that reinforce the neck, mane and leg silhouette ([Equine Helper](https://equinehelper.com/?p=20964); [Ankara Univ. study: bay most common at 56.6%](https://avesis.ankara.edu.tr/yayin/8b73a235-a6a6-4c03-b41a-d9850dce232e/color-and-marking-distribution-in-arabian-and-thoroughbred-horses)); a white hen needs the red comb and yellow beak and legs to carry the read, whereas a rust Rhode Island Red reads as chicken from colour alone ([Walden Effect](https://www.waldeneffect.org/blog/Most_popular_chicken_breeds/); [Tractor Supply](https://tractorsupply.com/tsc/cms/life-out-here/the-coop/poultry-breeds/rhode-island-red-nearly-everyone-s-favorite-breed)). The goat has no iconic colour (Saanen white, Toggenburg fawn with white stripes, Oberhasli bay, Boer red-headed white, Schleich's toy is brown-and-white), so it must lean on beard, horns and tail and take a colour that is not the sheep's white ([UKY Goat Discovery](https://afs.mgcafe.uky.edu/extension/4-h-youth/livestock/goat-discovery/breeds); [Schleich press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/schleich/pressreleases/lovely-accommodation-farm-animal-families-pets-a-warm-welcome-for-the-new-schleich-r-farm-world-characters-and-sets-3365289)). Three colourings are ruled out for collision: belted or spotted pigs (too close to Holstein), unmarked black horses (silhouette-only read), and white goats (collide with the sheep).

## Cuteness is a big head, eyes 45% down the face, and a blink every three seconds

The baby schema is the one part of this brief that rests on experiments rather than practice. Glocker and colleagues manipulated real infant photos within the natural anthropometric range and found that rounder faces, higher foreheads, larger eyes and smaller noses and mouths were rated cuter and elicited stronger caretaking motivation, and in fMRI drove greater nucleus accumbens activation ([Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/)). Borgi's eye-tracking showed that children aged 3–6 look longer at the eyes of high-infantile dog, cat and human faces and rate them cuter regardless of species ([ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140721100119.htm)); Little showed the same morph raises cuteness for cat faces ([Bath](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/manipulation-of-infant-like-traits-affects-perceived-cuteness-of-/)); Yao showed **eye size alone** raises cuteness across expressions and ages ([Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.674456/pdf)); and Miesler found the effect transfers even to car fronts with bigger headlights and does not habituate ([IJDesign](https://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1023)). Two qualifications matter for the engineer. First, there is an optimum: Kiss-Leizer applied the schema at 0–60% to car faces and found an inverted U, so pushing every feature to the limit is counterproductive ([MTAK](https://real.mtak.hu/60111/)). Second, the body features in Lorenz's list (short thick limbs, plump body, clumsy movement) have much weaker experimental backing than the facial ones; the main evidence is the teddy bear's evolution from long-snouted and long-limbed to snub-nosed and short-limbed, which a 2025 replication confirmed for forehead height ([Borredon & Dubourg 2025](https://edgar-dubourg.pages.dev/pdfs/borredon-dubourg-2025-jcc.pdf)).

Translated into head-count proportions, chibi practice converges on 2–3 heads of total height, with early Animal Crossing player characters literally half head and Tsum Tsum collapsing the body into a face-bearing rounded block ([Clip Studio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/es-es/articles/4806?org=1); [DX Town](https://pineight.com/mw/page/DX_Town_art_style.xhtml); [Mickey Blog](https://mickeyblog.com/?p=1756)). For a 24-voxel quadruped that means a head 10–12 voxels tall (45–50% of height), a body of similar height and legs of 3–5. The existing sheep is already inside this band: its head wedge is **10 rows tall on a 23-voxel figure (43%)**, its eye row sits at rows 17–18 of 13–22, which leaves **four rows of forehead above the eyes (40% of head height)**, and each eye is 3 × 2 voxels, in the "big" range for a 10-row head. The kawaii rule is that eyes sit on or below the horizontal midline of the face so that a vast forehead reads as vulnerable ([ZenPop](https://zenpop.jp/blog/post/5213/what-makes-a-drawing-kawaii)); the sheep's eye centre is 45% down from the crown, which is on the edge of that rule and should not be moved higher. The catchlight is what makes an eye read as alive rather than dead, and it must sit in the same position in both eyes or the character looks cross-eyed ([Photofocus](https://photofocus.com/photography/understanding-the-magic-of-catchlights/); [Autodesk community](https://origin-exchange.autodesk.com/how-to-draw-cute-eyes)); the sheep's single white voxel at the top-middle of each iris does this and is one of the parts every animal must share unchanged. Pixel artists warn that a single pixel wrong in an eye makes characters "look crazy, dumb, or completely stunned" ([NESdev](https://nesdev.nes.science/f21/t10923.xhtml)), which is the strongest argument for never letting a species override the eye part.

Babyfying has a stop line: shrink the baby-schema features (snout length, limb length, body length) but never the identity features. In voxels, "small nose" means a snout that is wide and flat (1 voxel proud) rather than long, so the pig keeps a 4 × 4 pink disc and the cow a 6 × 3 pink muzzle, both pressed onto the face. Teddy bears kept ears and fur colour while losing their snout, Pikachu kept ears, cheeks and tail while the body was rounded until the "mouse" label was an afterthought ([Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bear-culture-nature-heritage/out-of-the-wild-wood-and-into-our-beds-the-evolutionary-history-of-teddy-bears-and-the-natural-selection-of-deadly-cuteness/658587402AE79C17433CB3D9FA9E8C55); [Siliconera on Pikachu](https://www.siliconera.com/pikachu-originally-second-evolution-called-gorochu-large-fangs-two-horns/)). The practical test is to render the animal as a flat black silhouette; if it is not nameable, restore an identity feature before adding more roundness. The one species where the schema inverts is the horse: a foal's legs are already about 90% of adult length, so "baby horse" is read from long legs on a tiny body, not short legs ([Wikipedia: Foal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foal); [TheHorse.com](https://thehorse.com/154255/foal-morphology-here-come-those-legs/)); the horse therefore keeps the longest legs in the set and takes its cuteness from the oversized shared head on a short neck.

Motion is the cheapest cuteness the project has not yet spent. Behaviour changes cuteness ratings independently of appearance ([Wikipedia: Cuteness, citing Koyama 2006 and Karraker & Stern 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)), yuru-chara convention prizes movement that is "unique, unstable or awkward" ([Wikipedia: Yuru-kyara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuru-kyara)), and players explicitly ask for "a small hop" and "a cute head tilt when they notice something" ([r/PixelArt](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/PixelArt/comments/1jzr434/little_companion_i_need_to_finish)). The recipe for a rigid-part voxel rig is a blink every 2.5–4 s with random jitter, 100–150 ms closed, both eyes together (one developer's 2.5 s overlay is the only published number) ([GDevelop forum](https://forum.gdevelop.io/t/solved-making-my-character-blink/46268)); a 1-voxel breathing bob on a 1.5–2 s cycle; a 1-voxel head offset or 10° tilt on the neck pivot when the turn arrives; an ear flick of 20–30° for 2–3 frames; and a hop of 2–3 voxels with the root scaled one voxel shorter and one wider on takeoff and landing, volume preserved, with one extra wobble frame on landing. Squash and stretch "as long as volume is maintained" is the governing rule, and on a blocky model more than 1–2 voxels of deformation breaks the solid-toy read ([UW CSE 557](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse557/06wi/lectures/animation.pdf); [Wikipedia: Squash and stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_and_stretch)). The Crossy Road hop should be root scale, not per-voxel remodelling, which is what the Pixels film pipeline had to do and this renderer does not ([fxguide](https://www.fxguide.com/?p=59722)). Pair each hop or tilt with a short high-pitched squeak: Kringelbach's review treats high-frequency infant sounds as "auditory cuteness" ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4956347)).

## Voxel craft: nothing under two voxels, three tones, chamfer instead of steps

Minimum feature size is set by what Mojang gets away with and why. Its smallest attachments are the cow horn (1 × 3 × 1), the chicken wattle (2 × 2 × 2) and the horse ear (2 × 3 × 1), and they survive only because the horn is light on a dark head and the wattle is saturated red on white ([cow.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/cow.geo.json); [chicken.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/chicken.geo.json)). One Minecraft unit is about 1.56 voxels on the 0.04 m grid, so the equivalent floor here is a horn 1–2 voxels square and 4–5 tall, an ear 3–5 tall and 1–2 thick, and any feature that is 1 voxel in its smallest visible dimension must be a contrasting tone or it drops out at distance. Legs follow the same logic: Mojang never goes below a third of body width or 3 units absolute, and thin legs also break the "really obvious hit boxes and sharp edges" that Crossy Road's designers insisted on so hops could be judged ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/making-of-crossy-road/)). The sheep's 2 × 2 legs are acceptable because they are dark on cream and mostly hidden by fleece; on the pig, cow and horse, whose legs are exposed, use 3 × 3, and lift the hen from its current 1 × 1 orange posts to 2 × 2 with a 3-wide toe slab so the "two thin yellow legs" cue reads instead of vanishing.

Roundness is suggested, not modelled. Zach Soares names his own early failure as too much stair-stepping and not prioritising shape clarity, warns that procedural noise "will only cloud the design", recommends oversized heads as a readability hierarchy because dynamic shadows hide colour detail, and summarises the craft as "every voxel counts" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/voxel-art-reducing-the-greebles)); Sir Carma adds that "the best voxel art is when it stays simple" and that scale between assets is the most important thing to check ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/sir-carma-magica-voxel-was-like-having-infinite-legos-in-my-computer)). In practice that means knocking the corner columns off the head wedge (the sheep already does this), chamfering a 1-voxel edge on bodies and a 2-voxel edge on the cow barrel, making the belly one voxel wider than the shoulders, and keeping ears and tails 2 voxels thick where they can be. Wool, mane and feathers are 1–2 large lobes in two tones, never scattered voxels: Mojang's whole sheep fleece is one inflated box and its horse mane is one 2 × 2 strip. Because this renderer is flat-shaded Lambert with per-vertex colour and no textures, every Mojang trick that relies on texture alpha (the goat's zero-width beard plane, the reins, the baby head texture reuse) is unavailable, and every feature must be a real box of at least one voxel; the sheep's fleece shell is already a larger lobe cloud rather than an inflated duplicate, which is the right approach.

The part rig is the same for all six, following Mojang's bone tree and Soares' advice to use rigid pieces with slightly varied limb sizes to hide clipping: body root; head pivoted at the back of the jaw where it leaves the body (the sheep's `HEAD_PIVOT`); ears pivoted at their base on the head; snout, beak, horns, comb and beard rigid children of the head; four legs pivoted at the hip line (top-centre of each leg box); tail pivoted at the rump; the hen's wings pivoted at the top edge of the body sides ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/voxel-art-reducing-the-greebles); [horse_v3.geo.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/models/entity/horse_v3.geo.json)). The horse adds one bone the others lack, a neck between body and head, which is also how Mojang does it. The shared walk is one cosine per leg with diagonal pairs in phase, and the baby chicken is nothing more than the head scaled 2.0, which is a useful ceiling for how little animation a blocky body needs ([quadruped.animation.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/animations/quadruped.animation.json); [chicken.animation.json](https://github.com/Mojang/bedrock-samples/blob/main/resource_pack/animations/chicken.animation.json)). Keep legs inset one voxel from the body's side faces so an 80° swing does not interpenetrate; the current code keeps the ellipsoid from claiming any cell a leg or the head owns, and that rule must hold for every new part.

Colour tone rules are the most consistent across sources. Every material gets three shades (base, shadow, highlight); outlines and edges use a darker version of the base rather than black; shadows shift cooler in hue and highlights warmer, and the hue must move as well as the lightness or ramps go muddy ([Stardew Modding Wiki](https://stardewmodding.wiki.gg/wiki/How_to_make_pixel_art); [dev.to pixel-art guide](https://dev.to/autosprites/from-zero-to-mastery-a-complete-pixel-art-guide-for-modern-game-developers-205i); [Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/learn/color-palettes/2)). Skin and pink materials are the one exception, where shadows lean warm and reddish ([Pixnote](https://pixnote.net/en/learn/shading)). Pure black is wrong for a palette; cosy environments avoid it entirely and shift darks toward purple, and devlogs quote warm near-blacks like #1f1a16 and #0a0a08 ([SummerEngine art direction](https://www.claudepluginhub.com/skills/summerengine-summer/art-direction); [maxonice devlog](https://maxonice.com/posts/wl-devlog-art-direction); [r/Unity3D](https://red.127.pp.ua/r/Unity3D/comments/1l36hp8/testing_environment_lighting_in_a_cozy_blacksmith)). Two colours different in hue but equal in value "blend into an unreadable mush at small sizes", so the eyes and the signature feature must be the two highest-contrast marks on each animal ([Wayline](https://www.wayline.io/learn/color-palettes/1)). The budget per animal is 5–7 colours: a body triplet, the shared eye near-black and white, and one accent; since the Lambert renderer already darkens side faces, the shadow tone can often be dropped and only base plus highlight painted. The failure modes to test for are a mushy silhouette (fix: black-fill lineup of all six), uniform greeble noise (fix: fewer, bigger shapes), subtle gradients that die under flat shading (fix: contrasting tones), highlights everywhere so the face stops being the focal point (fix: brightest value reserved for eye whites and catchlights), and features under two voxels.

## A winter palette must separate three pale animals and spend one red

The cosy storybook look is a warm golden-hour bias with cool shadows, and the farm-tagged palettes on Lospec give usable anchors: Pumpkin Patch 15 (#9b5c43, #b18d56, #dc8b4d, #fdb156 against teals #304446, #3f5f65), Wheat Field (#305472, #2c313c shadows; #dfcd6d, #b78f43 lights) and Cozy Tones (#b35f47, #e3de88, #5c9974, #234a57) ([Lospec: Pumpkin Patch 15](https://lospec.com/palette-list/pumpkin-patch-15); [Lospec: Wheat Field](https://lospec.com/palette-list/wheat-field); [Lospec: Cozy Tones](https://lospec.com/palette-list/cozy-tones)). The unifying move is global: pull every animal's shadow tone toward one cool hue and every highlight toward one warm hue, so six different body colours read as one painted scene lit by one lantern. A Harvest Moon modder's remark that they "kept their white cows and chickens actually white" points at the real risk in this set: in a warm palette, white drifts to beige and the cow, sheep and hen converge ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/597)). The current `LOOK` table has exactly this problem, with cow #F4EFE6, hen #F7F1E4 and sheep cream #FAF6EC within a few points of each other. The fix is to push the cow's white cool (#F1ECE2 with a cool grey shadow #C9C6C0), the hen's body to a warm buff (#EAD9BD), and leave the sheep's cream between them, so the three pale animals separate by temperature. Snow dusting on backs must likewise be a cool white (#F7F4EE with a blue-grey shadow #D9DDE3) so it separates from fleece.

Saturation is where the set's one strong colour is spent. Desaturate bodies to roughly 25–45% saturation and allow a single saturated accent per animal; the hen's comb is the only strong red in the cast, which is why it reads, and it is the reason the cow's bell, the scarves and the pig's pink must all stay below it. Picture books supply the accessory vocabulary: in *Snowy Sunday* the farmer knits the animals coats, scarves, boots, shawls for the sheep, a coat and purple horn-warmers for the cow, and hats and tail-warmers for everyone ([Kirkus](https://kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/phyllis-root/snowy-sunday/print); [SLJ](https://www.schoollibraryjournal.com/review/snowy-sunday)); Jan Brett's winter farms add icicles on the chicken coop, a frozen pond and sleigh bells, and a stocking passed off as a hat ([Penguin Random House: The Snowy Nap](https://www.penguinrandomhouse.com/books/316441/the-snowy-nap-by-jan-brett-illustrated-by-jan-brett); [Penguin Australia: The Hat](https://www.penguin.com.au/books/the-hat-9781101652039)); Richard Scarry's cow "jangles her bell" ([Penguin Random House Canada](https://penguinrandomhouse.ca/books/66733/richard-scarrys-the-animals-of-farmer-jones-by-leah-gale-illustrated-by-richard-scarry/9780553498172)); and Hay Day's goats wear themed winter outfits layered on a fixed base ([Hay Day Wiki](https://hayday.fandom.com/wiki/Goat)). Two rules follow. The accessory is the animal's third colour and is drawn from one shared knit family (teal #3F5F65, mustard #DC8B4D, berry #B35F47) so the scarves themselves read as a set; and an accessory sits next to the signature feature, never over it, so no stocking cap on the hen's comb, no horn-warmers on the cow, no ear-muffs on the pig, and a scarf below rather than across the goat's beard. One collision is specific to this project: the sheep baseline already wears a red collar and bell, so the cow cannot own the bell as its signature. The cow keeps a bell for storybook reasons but on a brown leather strap with a brass bell, visibly different from the sheep's red collar, and its true signature is the Holstein patches with pale horns.

## Conclusion

The research reframes "design six cute animals" as "design one cute animal six ways". The sheep's existing head, eyes and part rig already satisfy the experimentally supported cuteness numbers, so the job is not to make each new species cuter but to protect that shared face while spending geometry on the three things that separate species in silhouette: a height-and-neck ladder, a single head attachment, and a tail direction. That is also what makes the set cheap: the engineer changes ellipsoid radii, leg boxes, pivots and one attachment per animal, and the sheep's lids, brows, mouths and poses animate every one of them unchanged.

The two findings that would be easy to miss are both collisions created by the existing baseline rather than by the research. The sheep already owns the red collar and bell, which forces the cow onto Holstein patches as its signature and onto a leather strap for its bell; and the sheep's cream sits so close to the current cow white and hen buff that the three pale animals will merge under the barn's warm light unless their temperatures are deliberately split. Fix those two, run the black-fill lineup, and the set will read at 20–30 voxels from any seat at the table.
