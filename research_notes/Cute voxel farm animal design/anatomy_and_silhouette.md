# Real anatomy and silhouette cues of six farm animals (pig, cow, goat, horse, hen, sheep baseline) for simplified blocky character design

Scope note: these notes are written for a ~20–30-voxel-tall blocky character. Anything finer than roughly 1/20 of body height (eyelashes, philtrum groove, dewclaws, individual feathers) is listed only as "cannot be shown" and is not treated as a design cue. Sources are a mix of artist tutorials (Tuts+, artnerds), extension/breed-standard pages (Kentucky 4-H goat breeds, poultry.extension.org, US Dairy), sanctuary/animal-comparison sites, Wikipedia, and toy-line listings. Many "how to draw" search hits were content-farm pages; only the reputable ones are cited.

## Key question 1: For each animal, what are the top 3 silhouette features that identify it at a glance?

### Takeaway
Each species has a small set of non-negotiable identifiers that survive at tiny size: pig = flat snout disc + curly tail + big floppy/pointed ears on a neckless barrel; cow = blocky horizontal body with horizontal leaf ears, blunt wide muzzle, tufted tail (plus Holstein patches and/or udder); goat = short up-flicked tail + beard + narrow up/back-swept horns on a slim body; horse = long neck + upright mane + long legs with a hanging full tail; hen = red comb/wattles + yellow beak + a low, round, tail-up body on two thin legs; sheep = fluffy wool mass with a bare face and a hanging (or docked) tail, no beard.

### Cited Findings

**Pig**
- A Tuts+ anatomy-based pig tutorial describes the pig as a body shaped "like a barrel with a head and legs"; the head is "huge, circular" and "slightly narrower than the rest of the body"; no neck is described at all (head is placed directly at the front of the body) — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794)
- Same source: the snout is a shape with a "flat bridge on top" that is "capped with a disc"; the lower jaw should be full ("Keep it fat!"); eyes are "small, oval, and simple" ("Keep the eyes small and simple"); ears are drawn as "floppy ears"; the tail is a "curly tail"; each hoof is split "down the middle to form two toes" — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794)
- Pig legs are "relatively short and sturdy" — [Craftwhack pig tutorial (cites the Tuts+ source)](https://craftwhack.com/how-to-draw-a-pig/)
- Ear carriage is breed-dependent: American Yorkshire is "White with erect ears"; American Landrace has "ears that tend to slant forward and droop a bit"; Danish Landrace has "very floppy ears" and a long snout; Angeln Saddleback has "large lop-ears" — [The Pink Pigs, Seven Pig Breeds to Know](https://thepinkpigs.com/pages/seven-pig-breeds-to-know)

**Cow**
- Tuts+ cow tutorial constructs the torso as "a long rectangle" bounding box, with the neck as "a triangle coming from the shoulder", a head oval at the triangle's tip, a separate muzzle oval, cheek ovals, horns rising from "a narrow oval on top of the head", a "slightly elevated" sacrum with the tail attached to it, and the udder "under the hips". The legs are "a vertical line slightly shorter than the height of the torso" — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050)
- Drawing guides agree the dewlap (loose fold of skin under the neck) and brisket are "crucial for a realistic look"; cow ears are "large, floppy, and leaf-shaped, sitting on the sides of the head" and point outward; the tail drops from the rump "reaching down to the hock" with "a small, bushy tuft at the end" — [search summary of cow-drawing guides; the only reputable one is Tuts+ bovines](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-animals-cows-and-other-bovines--cms-21683)
- Cartoons overwhelmingly draw cows black-and-white: TV Tropes catalogues shows (Family Guy, Peppa Pig, Zelda) and notes "many cartoons depict all cows as being black-and-white or brown-and-white and all bulls as being brown or black" — [TV Tropes: Bicolor Cows, Solid Color Bulls](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BicolorCowsSolidColorBulls) (page returned 403 on direct fetch; quoted from search snippet)

**Goat**
- "A healthy goat will have a short tail that usually sticks straight up or is held somewhat upright"; sheep tails "are longer than a goat's tail and hang straight down" — [Open Sanctuary: Distinguishing Sheep and Goats](https://opensanctuary.org/spot-the-difference-distinguishing-between-sheep-and-goats/)
- "Sheep don't grow beards, but the majority of goats do"; goat horns "point upwards and slightly back" and are narrower/shorter than sheep horns, which "curl at the sides of the head"; "generally to look at goats appear to be slimmer than sheep" — [A-Z Animals: Goat vs Sheep](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/)
- Tuts+ goat tutorial lists the goat identifiers it draws: "pointed horns" (adults) or "horn buds" (kids), a beard ("Both male and female goats have beards"), horizontal pupils, hocks pointing backwards, cloven hooves, "a short tail", coat "white to grey or black" — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948)
- Goats "often stand up on their hind legs to reach the tops of plants" — [A-Z Animals: Goat vs Sheep](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/)

**Horse**
- Head length is the standard unit: neck ≈ 1 head; body length ≈ 2.5–3 heads; chest depth, elbow-to-fetlock, shoulder-to-wither, ground-to-hock and hock-to-stifle each ≈ 1 head; height at withers ≈ point-of-shoulder-to-buttock length (i.e. the horse fits a square); the eye sits about one third of the way down the face — [artnerds: Drawing horses, general proportions](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions)
- Bay = "light reddish-brown to rich chocolate brown with black points"; "points" = "the mane, tail, lower legs, and ear rims"; chestnut = "A red coat with no black" — [Wikipedia: Equine coat color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equine_coat_color)
- Foals "can be distinguished from adult horses by their extremely long legs and small, slim bodies" — [Wikipedia: Foal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foal)

**Hen**
- "A chicken's comb and wattles are red, soft, and warm"; males have larger combs and wattles; "Most chickens have three toes projecting forward and one projecting back"; earlobes are red or white; males have sickle tail feathers and pointed hackles, females rounded hackles and no sickles — [poultry.extension.org: External Anatomy of Chickens](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/external-anatomy-of-chickens/)
- "The hen's comb remains relatively small and often flops to the side, while the rooster's comb stands erect and is significantly larger"; "Points only appear on single comb varieties, giving this comb style the look of a hair comb"; hens "only have main tail feathers and fluff" while roosters have long sickles — [Modern Farmer: 5 Differences Between Hens and Roosters](https://modernfarmer.com/2021/05/5-differences-between-hens-and-roosters/) and [My Pet Chicken](https://mypetchicken.com/blogs/our-blog/how-to-tell-a-rooster-from-a-hen)
- Drawing advice: a hen "is a heavy, low-slung animal built for scratching"; avoid a "swan neck" — the neck "should flow smoothly into the breast"; keep the comb low or "your hen will look like a rooster"; the comb is "a fleshy organ, not a piece of paper" so give it thickness — [Art in Context: How to Draw a Chicken](https://artincontext.org/how-to-draw-a-chicken/)

**Sheep (baseline)**
- Sheep have "a fleece of curly, crimped fur all over except the face"; sheep horns are "in the shape of a spiral"; most farm sheep have no horns; sheep tails "are long or docked and always hang downward"; sheep have a philtrum groove in the upper lip (goats do not) — [AnimalWised: Goat vs Sheep](https://animalwised.com/what-is-the-difference-between-a-goat-and-a-sheep-1364.html)
- Sheep "carry more weight across the loins and back"; "Most sheep have thick wool coats" — [A-Z Animals: Goat vs Sheep](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/)
- Sheep "do tend to have droopier, more folded ears, but not every breed follows this trend" — [Goat Journal: Difference Between Goats and Sheep](https://goatjournal.iamcountryside.com/ownership/do-you-know-the-difference-between-goats-and-sheep/)

### Inferences
- **Must-have identifiers at 20–30 voxels (my synthesis of the above):**
  - Pig: (1) flat circular snout disc on the front of the face, (2) curly tail, (3) large triangular ears (erect = Yorkshire read; forward-flopped = Landrace read). Fourth-tier: pink colour, no neck, short legs.
  - Cow: (1) long rectangular body with blunt wide muzzle (muzzle block as wide as the head), (2) horizontal leaf ears sticking straight out sideways (often with short horns above), (3) Holstein black-and-white patches and/or pink udder; the thin tufted tail is a strong fourth.
  - Goat: (1) short tail flicked UP, (2) beard under the chin, (3) narrow horns swept up/back (not curled). A slimmer, taller-legged body than the sheep helps.
  - Horse: (1) long neck set at ~45° with a mane strip along its top, (2) long legs (leg length roughly equals body depth), (3) long hanging tail of hair from the rump. A face blaze and black points add breed-read.
  - Hen: (1) red comb on top of head + red wattle below beak, (2) yellow/orange pointed beak, (3) low round body with a short raised tail fan, on two thin yellow legs with 3 forward toes.
  - Sheep (existing baseline): puffy wool body, bare smooth face/legs, small horizontal ears, hanging/docked tail, no beard, no horns.
- Because the sheep baseline is a fluffy blob, the single cheapest disambiguators are tail direction (goat UP vs sheep DOWN), a beard block (goat), and body texture (goat/pig/cow/horse are smooth, sheep is lumpy).
- The pig's lack of neck and the horse's very long neck sit at the opposite ends of the neck axis; cow and goat/sheep sit between. In voxels, neck length is one of the most efficient species variables.

### Gaps
- No source gave a formal, numeric "top-3" list; the ranking above is inferred from which features every tutorial and comparison page mentions first.
- The TV Tropes page could not be fetched directly (HTTP 403), so only its search snippet is cited.

## Key question 2: Typical adult proportions (head vs body, leg vs body depth, neck) and how juveniles differ

### Takeaway
Only the horse has well-documented head-unit proportions (body ≈ 2.5–3 heads, neck ≈ 1 head, foreleg elbow-to-fetlock ≈ 1 head, horse fits a square); the other species are documented qualitatively (pig: barrel + huge head + short legs; cow: long rectangle with legs slightly shorter than torso height; goat: slim; hen: low-slung). Juveniles follow Lorenz's baby schema (large head, big eyes, short thick limbs, plump body) except the foal, whose legs are already ~90% adult length and so reads as "all legs".

### Cited Findings

**Horse (adult)**
- Neck "approximately the same length as" the head; body length "about two and a half to three times the length of the head"; chest depth (girth) = 1 head; elbow-to-fetlock = 1 head; shoulder-to-wither = 1 head; ground-to-hock = 1 head; hock-to-stifle = 1 head; ground-to-withers "roughly equal to the distance between the point of its shoulder to its buttocks"; eyes "about one third of the way down the face"; mouth "about one sixth of the way along the length of the face"; point of buttock "slightly higher than the shoulder joint" — [artnerds: Drawing horses, general proportions](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions)
- Other tutorials in search results conflict on body-in-heads (ranging from 1.5–2 to 4.5–5 heads); the 2.5–3 figure comes from the most anatomy-literate source and is consistent with the 1770 veterinary reference it cites — [search summary of horse-proportion tutorials](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions)

**Horse (foal)**
- "A newborn foal's legs are almost as long (90%) as those of an adult horse" — [search summary citing Wikipedia Foal / TheHorse.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foal)
- Foals are "extremely long legs and small, slim bodies"; pony foals "are slimmer and have proportionally longer legs than their adult parents" — [Wikipedia: Foal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foal)
- Foal legs are proportionally longer than an adult animal of the same weight; a Univ. of Chile study (Bruno Grossi) links this to keeping pace with the herd at migration speeds — [TheHorse.com: Foal Morphology, Here Come Those Legs](https://thehorse.com/154255/foal-morphology-here-come-those-legs/)
- Older veterinary text: before the 24th week of gestation limb bones are nearly adult relative length, and "a later increase in the cannon bones explains to a large extent the great length of the foal's legs at birth" — [Chest of Books, Rate of Growth in the Horse (J. Wortley Axe)](https://mail.chestofbooks.com/animals/horses/Health-Disease-Treatment-4/The-Rate-Of-Growth-In-The-Horse-1-Part-3.html)

**Pig (adult)**
- Body "like a barrel with a head and legs"; head "huge, circular" and "slightly narrower than the rest of the body"; shoulders a vertical oval, hips a circle; no neck; the perspective guide sits at "about half of the pig's torso's height" — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794)
- Legs "relatively short and sturdy" — [Craftwhack](https://craftwhack.com/how-to-draw-a-pig/)
- American Landrace: "very long body and long snout"; Berkshire: "Low to the ground with short legs"; Tamworth: "elongated head and body" — [The Pink Pigs](https://thepinkpigs.com/pages/seven-pig-breeds-to-know)

**Cow (adult)**
- Torso = "a long rectangle" cut into quarters; chest oval in the middle; legs "a vertical line slightly shorter than the height of the torso"; sacrum "slightly elevated"; udder "under the hips"; neck is "a triangle coming from the shoulder" — [Tuts+ How to Draw a Cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050)
- Tail reaches "down to the hock" — [cow-drawing guides summary](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-animals-cows-and-other-bovines--cms-21683)

**Goat (adult and kid)**
- Adult neck is "a simple elongated shape"; adult has "a short tail"; forelimbs "thicker in the knee area"; goats "appear to be slimmer than sheep" — [Tuts+ Goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948); [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/)
- Kid: the head is "relatively big in comparison to the body" and "shorter than the head of an adult goat"; the neck is shorter; no horns, only "horn buds" ("small bumps on the top of the head, between the ears and above the eyes"); leave "enough space for the forehead"; real goat eyes are "relatively small" but the tutorial draws them "slightly bigger" for cuteness — [Tuts+ Goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948)

**Hen**
- "a heavy, low-slung animal built for scratching at the earth"; the neck "should flow smoothly into the breast"; a hen's comb is "usually smaller and might even flop to one side" — [Art in Context chicken tutorial](https://artincontext.org/how-to-draw-a-chicken/)

**Juvenile / baby schema (all species)**
- Lorenz's Kindchenschema: "large head, high and protruding forehead, large eyes, chubby cheeks, small nose and mouth, short and thick extremities and plump body shape" — [Glocker et al. 2009, PNAS / PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2690007); [Kawaguchi & Waller](https://irep.ntu.ac.uk/id/eprint/51599/1/1905604_Kawaguchi.pdf)
- Cute-animal drawing guides: head "about 1/4 to 1/3 of the total height" (some say "roughly twice the size of the torso"); limbs "short, chunky"; "Prioritise round forms, avoiding sharp, angular lines"; and the explicit exception: foals' legs "are long and skinny, so the cute-proportion approach applies less to them" — [Clip Studio Tips: drawing cute animals](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/7129) and search summary of baby-animal tutorials

### Inferences
- Practical voxel ratios (derived, not measured): horse body ≈ 3 heads long, legs ≈ chest depth, neck ≈ 1 head; cow body ≈ 2–2.5 heads long with legs ≈ 0.8 × torso height; pig body ≈ 2 heads long with head ≈ 0.8 × body height and legs ≈ 0.4 × body height; goat ≈ cow-ish proportions but narrower body and lighter legs; hen body ≈ 1.5 heads with legs ≈ 0.5 × body height.
- To "cutify" any adult farm animal for a 20–30-voxel character, follow baby schema: head ≈ 1/3 of total height, legs ≈ 1/4 or less, eyes big and low on a tall forehead, everything rounded. The exception is a foal, where keeping legs long and the body tiny is what reads "baby horse".
- A kid/lamb reads juvenile by removing horns (horn buds only) and shortening the muzzle; a calf by removing the udder and horns and shrinking the muzzle; a piglet by a smaller snout disc and rounder body; a chick by a round fluffy yellow ball with no comb/wattles and a tiny beak (chick colour inferred from widespread convention; see Gaps).

### Gaps
- No reputable source gave numeric head-unit proportions for pig, cow, goat, sheep or hen; several content-farm pages gave conflicting numbers (e.g. pig "2.5 heads" vs "2/3 body, 1/3 head") and are not relied on.
- No source gave numeric leg-length percentages for calf, lamb, kid or piglet comparable to the foal's 90% figure.
- No source was found describing chick proportions or the yellow-chick convention; treat chick notes as inference.

## Key question 3: How do ears differ in shape, size and set across pig, cow, goat, horse and sheep?

### Takeaway
Pig ears are large and triangular, either erect (Yorkshire) or flopped forward over the eyes (Landrace); cow ears are large, leaf-shaped and held out horizontally from the sides of the head; goat ears range from erect-and-forward (Swiss breeds), through long pendulous "bell" ears (Nubian/Boer), to almost absent (LaMancha); horse ears are small, pointed and upright on top of the head (the ear rim is one of the "points"); sheep ears are typically small and horizontal-to-droopy, but breed-variable.

### Cited Findings
- Pig: Yorkshire "erect ears"; American Landrace ears "slant forward and droop a bit"; Danish Landrace "very floppy ears"; Angeln Saddleback "large lop-ears"; Tamworth "pointed ears"; Berkshire ears "large" — [The Pink Pigs](https://thepinkpigs.com/pages/seven-pig-breeds-to-know). Tuts+ draws "floppy ears" by default — [Tuts+ Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794). Livestock databases classify ear type as "erect, drooping, or lop-eared" — [DAGRIS](https://dagris.info/node/2196)
- Cow: ears "large, floppy, and leaf-shaped, sitting on the sides of the head" and pointing outward; "An angry cow has pinned-back ears. A curious one has them forward" — [cow-drawing guide summary](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-animals-cows-and-other-bovines--cms-21683)
- Goat, per Kentucky 4-H breed descriptions: Alpine "erect, medium-sized"; Saanen and Toggenburg "erect, carried forward"; Oberhasli "erect, medium length"; Pygmy "erect, medium-sized, firm"; Nubian "pendulous: very long, 'bell' shaped, hanging close to the head" with a "Roman" convex nose; Boer "large, floppy ears (lop ears)"; LaMancha "very small flaps, nearly earless" (gopher ≈ 1 in, elf ≈ 2 in); Angora "heavy and drooping" — [UKY Goat Discovery: Breeds](https://afs.mgcafe.uky.edu/extension/4-h-youth/livestock/goat-discovery/breeds). Oberhasli ears are "short erect ears set low and pointed forward" — [search summary of ADGA/AGS standards](https://www.americangoatsociety.com/breed-standards.php)
- Horse: the ear rims are among the "points" (mane, tail, lower legs, ear rims) that go black on a bay — [Wikipedia: Equine coat color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equine_coat_color)
- Sheep vs goat ears: sources conflict. One says sheep "tend to have droopier, more folded ears, but not every breed follows this trend"; another claims goats have longer ears that hang down more while sheep ears "stick outwards" — [Goat Journal](https://goatjournal.iamcountryside.com/ownership/do-you-know-the-difference-between-goats-and-sheep/) vs [search summary]. Open Sanctuary and A-Z Animals do not use ears as a diagnostic at all — [Open Sanctuary](https://opensanctuary.org/spot-the-difference-distinguishing-between-sheep-and-goats/)

### Inferences
- Ear set is a cheap, high-value voxel variable: horse = 2 small upright wedges on top of the head; cow = 2 wide flat slabs sticking straight out sideways at eye level (below/beside horns); pig = 2 large triangles, either upright or folded forward (forward-fold reads "cute piglet", upright reads "Yorkshire/cartoon pig"); goat = 2 medium ears, either upright-forward (Swiss/Alpine look) or long hanging slabs (Nubian look); sheep = 2 small horizontal nubs barely clearing the wool.
- Because the sheep baseline likely already has small horizontal ears, the goat should NOT reuse them: either make them clearly upright (Alpine) or clearly long-and-hanging (Nubian) so the ear alone separates the two.
- Horse ears must be small; oversizing them turns the silhouette toward donkey/mule.

### Gaps
- No source quantified ear length relative to head length for any species.
- Sheep ear set is genuinely breed-variable and the comparison sites disagree; no breed-standard page for sheep ears was consulted.

## Key question 4: Most iconic coat colours/patterns per species, and which read best at low resolution

### Takeaway
The iconic palette is Holstein black-and-white patches for the cow (Holsteins are 86% of US dairy cows), plain pink for the pig (white breeds like Yorkshire/Landrace read pink), bay or chestnut with a white face blaze for the horse, white with red comb (Leghorn) or rust-red (Rhode Island Red) for the hen, white/cream (Saanen) or brown-and-white for the goat, and white wool with a bare (often black) face for the sheep.

### Cited Findings
- Cow: Holsteins "make up 86% of U.S. dairy cows"; Jersey nearly 8%, Brown Swiss 0.8%, Ayrshire 0.2%, Guernsey 0.2%; the article attributes the iconic image to there being "just so darn many of them" — [US Dairy: Why do people think all dairy cows are black and white?](https://www.usdairy.com/news-articles/why-do-people-think-all-dairy-cows-are-black-and-white). Cartoons "feature Holsteins on the rare occasions when cows are called for" — [The Western Producer editorial](https://www.producer.com/opinion/holsteins-take-over-corporate-world-editorial-notebook). Holstein spotting is driven by variants of KIT and MITF genes (Massey University, Nov 2025) — [Rural News Group](https://ruralnewsgroup.co.nz/dairy-news/dairy-farm-health/holstein-coat-genes-discovery-massey-university)
- Pig: Yorkshire "White"; Landrace "White coat with fine white hair"; Tamworth "red or 'ginger'"; Berkshire "primarily black with a few white spots on their face, legs, or tail"; Angeln Saddleback "black body that typically has a white band going all the way around it"; Gloucestershire Old Spot "primarily white skin and hair with black spots" — [The Pink Pigs](https://thepinkpigs.com/pages/seven-pig-breeds-to-know). Hampshire is the US belted (black with white belt) breed per study flashcards (unverified against registry) — [search summary](https://livestockoftheworld.com/pigs)
- Horse: bay and chestnut are the most common colours, followed by grey and black; bay = reddish-brown body with black mane, tail, lower legs; chestnut = red with same-shade mane/tail — [Horse Fact Book](https://horsefactbook.com/guides/horse-colors) and [Wikipedia: Equine coat color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equine_coat_color). A study of 101 Arabians and 206 Thoroughbreds found bay most common at 56.6% — [Ankara Univ. study](https://avesis.ankara.edu.tr/yayin/8b73a235-a6a6-4c03-b41a-d9850dce232e/color-and-marking-distribution-in-arabian-and-thoroughbred-horses). Face markings: bald face (eye to eye), blaze (broad strip between eyes), stripe (narrower), star (forehead patch), snip (muzzle patch); leg markings coronet < pastern < sock (mid cannon) < stocking (to hock) — [Equine Helper: 21 Common Horse Colors](https://equinehelper.com/?p=20964). White markings "are determined by separate genes and are not related to the bay coloration" — [search summary]
- Hen: White Leghorns "are known for their white feathers, bright red combs, and white earlobes" and are "the most common layer breed" in an extension guide; Rhode Island Red plumage "ranges from a lustrous deep red to almost black; the tail is mostly black"; RIR ranks first in merged US backyard-breed surveys — [Walden Effect: Most popular chicken breeds](https://www.waldeneffect.org/blog/Most_popular_chicken_breeds/); [Tractor Supply: Rhode Island Red](https://tractorsupply.com/tsc/cms/life-out-here/the-coop/poultry-breeds/rhode-island-red-nearly-everyone-s-favorite-breed). "Typically, chicken breeds with white ear lobes lay white eggs and chickens with red ear lobes lay brown eggs" — [search summary]. Schleich's Plymouth Rock figure has "striking black and white feathers" (barred) — [Schleich press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/schleich/pressreleases/lovely-accommodation-farm-animal-families-pets-a-warm-welcome-for-the-new-schleich-r-farm-world-characters-and-sets-3365289)
- Goat: Saanen "White or light cream, white preferred"; Toggenburg "Light fawn to dark chocolate with white markings" (facial stripes, legs); Oberhasli "red bay (chamois) with black markings"; Boer "Red head and ears, white body, white face blaze"; Nubian "black, red, or tan most common"; Pygmy "grizzled (agouti) most predominant"; Tennessee Fainting "Black and white predominant" — [UKY Goat Discovery: Breeds](https://afs.mgcafe.uky.edu/extension/4-h-youth/livestock/goat-discovery/breeds). Tuts+ gives goat coat as "white to grey or black" — [Tuts+ Goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948). Schleich's current farm goat is "brown and white" — [Schleich press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/schleich/pressreleases/lovely-accommodation-farm-animal-families-pets-a-warm-welcome-for-the-new-schleich-r-farm-world-characters-and-sets-3365289)
- Sheep: Schleich's Valais Blacknose figure has "white wool and classic black face" — [Schleich press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/schleich/pressreleases/lovely-accommodation-farm-animal-families-pets-a-warm-welcome-for-the-new-schleich-r-farm-world-characters-and-sets-3365289); Hereford (red body, white face) and Aberdeen Angus (black) also appear in Schleich's cattle line — [eBay Schleich farm listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/234593783811)

### Inferences
- Low-resolution legibility ranking (my judgement): Holstein patches are the single best colour cue in the set because 3–5 large irregular black blobs on white read at any size; pink is unique to the pig in this roster so flat pink alone identifies it; bay (brown body + black mane/tail/lower legs + white blaze) gives the horse three contrasting zones that reinforce neck/mane/leg silhouette; a white hen needs the red comb/wattle and yellow beak/legs to carry the read (the body colour does little), whereas a rust-red hen reads as "chicken" from colour alone; the goat has no universally iconic colour, so lean on shape (beard/horns/tail) and pick a colour that differs from the sheep's white (brown-and-white, grey, or tan with a white blaze).
- Avoid: belted/spotted pigs (too close to Holstein), black horses with no markings (silhouette-only read), white goats (collide with the white sheep baseline).
- Keep the Holstein black patches off the face center and legs-lower-half so the pink muzzle and udder stay visible.

## Key question 5: Which tail, horn, beard, mane, comb and wattle shapes are canonical in cartoon/toy depictions, and why do they read well?

### Takeaway
Cartoons and toys keep the handful of appendages that are species-unique and high-contrast: pig corkscrew tail, cow horns-plus-horizontal-ears and tufted tail (and udder/bell on cows), goat beard plus up-swept horns and up-flicked tail, horse crest mane and full hanging tail, hen single comb (a "hair comb" with points) plus wattles. These read well because each is a simple geometric add-on (spiral, wedge, strip, fan, scallop) that changes the outline rather than the interior detail.

### Cited Findings
- Pig: the curly tail and floppy ears are the two appendages the Tuts+ pig tutorial adds beyond body/head; the snout is explicitly "capped with a disc" — [Tuts+ Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794)
- Cow: horns built from "a narrow oval on top of the head"; udder "under the hips"; tail on the elevated sacrum — [Tuts+ Cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050). Tail is "relatively thin with a tuft of hair at the end" — [cow-drawing guides summary](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-animals-cows-and-other-bovines--cms-21683). Cartoon convention: cows bicolour (black/white or brown/white), bulls solid — [TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/BicolorCowsSolidColorBulls)
- Goat: horns "two curved lines rising from the top of the head", "taper the horns slightly at the tips", "slightly curved and symmetrical"; beard sketched "from the chin" with outer edges extended "to create a fuller, rounded shape"; from some angles "only one ear shows because the horns hide the other" — [search summary of goat tutorials incl. Clip Studio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/8647) and [Art in Context goat tutorial](https://artincontext.org/how-to-draw-a-goat). Goat horns "point upwards and slightly back"; sheep horns "curl at the sides of the head"; goat tail "short... pointing upwards" — [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/). "Both male and female goats have beards" — [Tuts+ Goat](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-goat-step-by-step--cms-29948)
- Horse: the mane, tail, lower legs and ear rims are the "points" that carry black on a bay, which is why they are visually emphasised — [Wikipedia: Equine coat color](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equine_coat_color); neck ≈ 1 head length gives the mane a long strip to sit on — [artnerds](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions)
- Hen: the single comb's points give it "the look of a hair comb"; hen comb "relatively small and often flops to the side"; hens have only "main tail feathers and fluff", roosters add sickles — [Modern Farmer](https://modernfarmer.com/2021/05/5-differences-between-hens-and-roosters/); comb and wattles "are red, soft, and warm"; 3 toes forward, 1 back — [poultry.extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/external-anatomy-of-chickens/); give the comb "some thickness... It's a fleshy organ, not a piece of paper"; the wattle is "a little uneven circle from the base of the beak to the bottom of the head" — [Art in Context chicken](https://artincontext.org/how-to-draw-a-chicken/)
- Sheep: wool drawn with "quick, short, curved strokes"; "The farther away from the guide lines you draw the wool, the thicker the coat will look" — [Drawing For All cartoon sheep](https://www.drawingforall.net/how-to-draw-a-cartoon-sheep/); sheep horns are spiral and most farm sheep are hornless — [AnimalWised](https://animalwised.com/what-is-the-difference-between-a-goat-and-a-sheep-1364.html)
- Toy lines: Fisher-Price Little People Farm Animal Friends set contains llama, chicken, cow, goat, horse, pig, sheep and dog, i.e. toy makers consider goat and sheep distinct enough to sell side-by-side at toddler scale — [Mattel GFL21](https://shop.mattel.com/products/fisher-price-little-people-farm-animal-friends-gfl21). Schleich Farm World sells named cattle breeds (Holstein, Jersey, Hereford, Aberdeen Angus), a brown-and-white goat, Plymouth Rock and Silkie chickens, and a Valais Blacknose sheep — [eBay Schleich listing](https://www.ebay.de/itm/234593783811); [Schleich press release](https://www.mynewsdesk.com/schleich/pressreleases/lovely-accommodation-farm-animal-families-pets-a-warm-welcome-for-the-new-schleich-r-farm-world-characters-and-sets-3365289)

### Inferences
- Voxel recipes for the canonical appendages (derived from the above):
  - Pig tail: a 3–4-voxel "S"/spiral sticking up-and-back from the rump; it is the only spiral in the roster, so it is unmistakable.
  - Cow tail: a 1-voxel-thick rod hanging to the hock, ending in a 2-voxel dark tuft; horns: two short outward-curving wedges above the horizontal ears (even on a cow, cartoons keep them); udder: a pink box under the belly with 2–4 teat voxels; a bell on a collar is a cartoon-only add that strengthens the read.
  - Goat tail: a 2-voxel stub pointing UP at ~45–90°; beard: a 2–3-voxel block hanging under the chin; horns: two thin rods angled up and back (not curled, not outward).
  - Horse mane: a 1-voxel-wide strip along the top of the neck from poll to withers, plus a forelock; tail: a hanging slab/rod of hair from the rump to roughly hock level; ears: 2 tiny upright wedges.
  - Hen comb: a red 1-voxel-thick scalloped plate on top of the head (3–4 bumps = "hair comb" read), kept SMALL and optionally tilted/flopped to stay "hen" not "rooster"; wattles: 1–2 red voxels under the beak; tail: a short upward fan of 3–5 voxels; feet: 3 forward toes.
  - Sheep (baseline, presumably already present): fluffy lumpy body with a smooth face/legs; keep it hornless and beardless so goat horns/beard stay exclusive.
- These shapes read at low resolution because each changes the outer silhouette (spiral, up-stub, down-rod, crest strip, scallop) rather than relying on interior shading, and each is unique within the farm set.

### Gaps
- No academic or design-industry source was found explaining *why* specific cartoon appendages read well; the explanation is inference from silhouette theory.
- Picture-book illustration conventions were not directly surveyed (no fetchable source on, e.g., which features children's illustrators keep).
- The TV Tropes trope pages could not be fetched (403); cow/bull convention relies on the search snippet.

## Key question 6: How does each animal differ from the sheep baseline and from each other?

### Takeaway
Against a fluffy, hornless, beardless, tail-down white sheep, the cheapest separators are: goat = smooth coat + tail up + beard + up/back horns; pig = pink neckless barrel + snout disc + corkscrew tail; cow = long flat rectangle + horizontal ears + Holstein patches + udder; horse = long neck with mane + long legs + hanging hair tail; hen = two-legged, red comb/wattle, yellow beak and feet.

### Cited Findings
- Goat vs sheep diagnostic features (tail up vs down, beard, slimmer build, horn shape, hair vs wool, philtrum) — [Open Sanctuary](https://opensanctuary.org/spot-the-difference-distinguishing-between-sheep-and-goats/); [A-Z Animals](https://a-z-animals.com/animals/comparison/goat-vs-sheep/); [AnimalWised](https://animalwised.com/what-is-the-difference-between-a-goat-and-a-sheep-1364.html)
- Pig: barrel body, huge head, no neck, snout disc, curly tail, floppy ears — [Tuts+ Pig](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-pig--cms-30794)
- Cow: long rectangle torso, triangle neck, horns on a narrow oval, udder under hips, legs slightly shorter than torso height — [Tuts+ Cow](https://design.tutsplus.com/tutorials/how-to-draw-a-cow--cms-31050)
- Horse: neck ≈ 1 head, body 2.5–3 heads, square outline — [artnerds](https://artnerds.substack.com/p/drawing-horses-general-proportions)
- Hen: red comb and wattles, 3+1 toes, hen comb small and may flop — [poultry.extension.org](https://poultry.extension.org/articles/poultry-anatomy/external-anatomy-of-chickens/); [Modern Farmer](https://modernfarmer.com/2021/05/5-differences-between-hens-and-roosters/)
