# Cuteness principles for stylised (voxel) farm animals: baby schema, chibi proportions, eyes, motion, colour, species readability

Scope note: these notes cover the perceptual science behind "cute" plus practitioner rules of thumb from chibi/kawaii/mascot design, each translated into what can be done with a 20–30 voxel tall blocky character. Where sources give no number, the voxel translation is marked as an inference.

## Key question 1: What are the baby-schema (Kindchenschema) features and what evidence supports them?

### Takeaway
Lorenz's baby schema (large head relative to body, high/bulging forehead, large low-set eyes, round protruding cheeks, small nose and mouth, short thick limbs, plump/soft body, clumsy movement) is one of the best-replicated findings in cuteness research: experimentally raising those features in infant, adult, cat and dog faces, and even car fronts and teddy bears, raises cuteness ratings, caretaking motivation, and reward-system activation.

### Cited Findings
- Baby schema is defined in the research literature as "a set of facial and body features that make a creature appear 'cute' and activate ('release') in others the motivation to care for it"; the concept originates with Konrad Lorenz (1943/1949, collected in *Studies in Animal and Human Behavior*, 1971), who proposed features including large eyes and chubby cheeks — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)
- Glocker et al. (2009, *Ethology* 115(3):257–263) produced high- and low-baby-schema versions of real infant photos: the high-schema versions had "a rounder face, higher forehead, larger eyes, and smaller nose and mouth"; low-schema versions had "narrow face, low forehead, small eyes, large nose and mouth". Manipulations were based on anthropometric measurements from 40 unmanipulated infants, so they stayed within the normal range. High-baby-schema infants were rated cuter and elicited stronger caretaking motivation than both unmanipulated and low-schema faces; the authors call this "the first experimental proof of the baby schema effects in actual infant faces". Sample reported as 122 undergraduate raters — [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11); [Wiley abstract](https://onlinelibrary-wiley-com-443.webvpn.zafu.edu.cn/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1439-0310.2008.01603.x)
- Glocker et al. (2009, *PNAS*, "Baby schema modulates the brain reward system in nulliparous women") used fMRI and found faces with more baby-schema content produced greater nucleus accumbens activation (reward/motivation) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3260535/); [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)
- Borgi et al. (2014, *Frontiers in Psychology*): children aged 3–6 looked longer at the eyes of "high infantile" dog, cat and human faces than "low infantile" ones and rated them cuter; infantile features were "a rounder face, a higher forehead, bigger eyes, a smaller nose and a smaller mouth". Quote: "Independently of the species viewed, children in our study spent more time looking at images with a higher degree of these baby-like features." Observers focused on the eye region rather than nose/mouth — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140721100119.htm); [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)
- Little (2012, *Ethology* 118:775–782, "Manipulation of infant-like traits affects perceived cuteness of infant, adult and cat faces"): morphing faces toward an infant shape (small jaw, large forehead) increased cuteness ratings for human infant, human adult AND cat faces; infants and cats were rated cutest, adults least — [Bath research portal](https://researchportal.bath.ac.uk/en/publications/manipulation-of-infant-like-traits-affects-perceived-cuteness-of-/); [Stirling repository](https://dspace.stir.ac.uk/handle/1893/17928)
- Yao et al. (2022, *Frontiers in Psychology*, "Eye Size Affects Cuteness in Different Facial Expressions and Ages"): manipulating eye size alone, with paired comparisons and a 7-point scale, faces with enlarged eyes were rated cuter than both unmanipulated and reduced eyes across positive, neutral and negative expressions and across adult and infant faces — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.674456/pdf); [BIT listing](https://pure.bit.edu.cn/zh/publications/eye-size-affects-cuteness-in-different-facial-expressions-and-age)
- Alley (1981, *Developmental Psychology*) altered head shape of infant line drawings with a cardioidal (heart-shaped, top-heavy) transformation and 25 undergraduates rated them; Jones et al. (1995, *Current Anthropology*) describe juvenile features qualitatively as smaller snouts, higher foreheads, larger eyes — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)
- Baby schema transfers to non-faces: Miesler, Leder & Herrmann (2011, *International Journal of Design* 5(3):17–30) edited car fronts (e.g. larger "eye" headlights) and measured facial EMG plus ratings (n = 57); reactions were more positive to the babyfaced car fronts than originals, the effect was stable over repeated exposure (no fast habituation), and cuteness ratings of car fronts, though lower, were comparable in pattern to human faces — [IJDesign](https://www.ijdesign.org/index.php/IJDesign/article/view/1023); [Pacific Standard](https://psmag.com/?p=42497)
- There is an optimum, not "more is always better": Kiss-Leizer (2017) tested 13 car-face images with baby-schema applied at 0% to 60% and found "a particular degree of application of the baby schema made ratings of cuteness the highest" (i.e. an inverted-U) — [MTAK repository](https://real.mtak.hu/60111/)
- Hinde & Barden (1985, "The evolution of the teddy bear") argued that teddy bears evolved over 1903–1985 "from a long-snouted, long-limbed bear to a cute, snub-nosed, baby-like creature" to match sign stimuli that release nurturant behaviour; a 2025 pre-registered replication (Borredon & Dubourg) found forehead height of teddy bears significantly increased over time; the original study used only 15 bears — [Borredon & Dubourg 2025 PDF](https://edgar-dubourg.pages.dev/pdfs/borredon-dubourg-2025-jcc.pdf); [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bear-culture-nature-heritage/out-of-the-wild-wood-and-into-our-beds-the-evolutionary-history-of-teddy-bears-and-the-natural-selection-of-deadly-cuteness/658587402AE79C17433CB3D9FA9E8C55)
- Morris, Reddy & Bunting (1995) critiqued the teddy-bear result, arguing the evolution "is thus apparently not driven by the ostensible consumer, the young child" (adults buy them) — [Portsmouth research portal](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/the-survival-of-the-cutest-whos-responsible-for-the-evolution-of-/)
- Kringelbach et al. (2016, *Trends in Cognitive Sciences* 20:545–558, "On Cuteness: Unlocking the Parental Brain and Beyond") propose cuteness extends beyond vision to sounds and smells; infant laughs/babbles are "auditory cuteness" (high-frequency, pure-tone-like sounds); the orbitofrontal cortex responds to an infant face within ~130 ms; both men and women, including non-parents, expend extra effort to look longer at cute infant faces — [Oxford psych page](https://www.psych.ox.ac.uk/team/mlk/publication_modal/625455); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4956347); [Neuroscience News](https://neurosciencenews.com/parent-behavior-baby-cuteness-4385)
- Sherman, Haidt & Coan (2009): viewing cute images (puppies, kittens) improved fine-motor carefulness on the game Operation; Sherman & Haidt proposed cuteness motivates social engagement and primes affiliative tendencies — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness); [PMC Sherman/Haidt](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3458879)
- Nittono (2012) found viewing cute images narrowed attentional focus and increased accuracy; Nittono characterises the kawaii response as moderate arousal, strong approach motivation and "social orientation". Steinnes et al. (2019) argue cuteness typically evokes kama muta (being moved), more so when cute targets interact affectionately — [Frontiers 2019 Steinnes](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00387/pdf); [MDPI 2025 review](https://mdpi.com/2673-8392/5/3/146)
- Behaviour affects perceived cuteness independent of looks: Koyama et al. (2006) and Karraker & Stern (1990) found an infant's behaviour and beliefs about its personality alter cuteness ratings — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)

### Inferences
- The experimentally validated feature set is consistent across ~15 years of studies: (1) round/wide face, (2) high forehead (eyes low relative to total head height), (3) big eyes, (4) small nose and mouth, (5) small jaw. Body features (short thick limbs, plump body, clumsy movement) are part of Lorenz's original list but have far less experimental testing; the teddy bear work (snout and limb shortening) is the main indirect evidence.
- Because the effect transfers to cats, dogs, cars and teddy bears, it should transfer to a voxel cow/pig/chicken; the features are "sign stimuli", not species-specific.
- The Kiss-Leizer inverted-U means pushing every feature to the max is counterproductive; keep manipulations in the "plausible" range (Glocker stayed within natural infant variation).
- Eye-tracking (Borgi) shows viewers lock onto the eyes, so eyes are the highest-leverage voxels in the whole model.
- Voxel translation (20–30 voxels tall): head block should be the single biggest mass; eyes placed in the lower half of the head; nose/mouth as 1-voxel or absent; limbs 2–4 voxels long and at least 2 wide; no neck or a 1-voxel neck.

### Gaps
- I could not obtain the Glocker 2009 *Ethology* full text (PDF fetched but not text-extractable), so the exact percentage amounts of forehead/eye enlargement and the mean rating differences are not quoted here.
- The Borredon & Dubourg 2025 replication's eye-size and head-roundness results (as opposed to forehead) were not visible in the snippet.
- Lorenz's verbatim 1943 list (including "short and thick extremities", "clumsy movements") is widely paraphrased in textbooks but I did not retrieve a primary quotation; treat the body/motion items as Lorenz's hypotheses with weaker empirical backing than the facial ones.
- Yao et al. (2022): the percentage by which eyes were enlarged is in the Methods but the full text returned 404.

## Key question 2: What head-to-body ratios do chibi/mascot designers use, and how do Nintendo, Sanrio, Animal Crossing, Pokémon and Tsum Tsum handle proportion?

### Takeaway
Chibi practice converges on 2 to 3 "heads" total height (head = 1/2 to 1/3 of the figure), with "super chibi" at 2 heads; Animal Crossing's early-era player characters were literally half head, and Tsum Tsum removes the body almost entirely (rounded-rectangle "head with a face"). No official Nintendo/Sanrio proportion sheets were found.

### Cited Findings
- Clip Studio chibi guides count proportions in heads: a body of one head gives a 2-head chibi; a body of two heads gives a 3-head chibi; the body ratio "changes drastically to 1:4 (small child) up to 1:2 (super chibi)"; for a 3-head chibi "one head is the length that roughly spans from the chin to the navel" — [Clip Studio Tips: chibi body ratio](https://tips.clip-studio.com/es-es/articles/4806?org=1); [Clip Studio: Easy steps to chibi](https://clipstudio.net/how-to-draw/archives/155423)
- Commonly used chibi ratios are 2, 2.5 and 3 heads tall, versus about 7 heads for regular anime characters; some guides broaden this to 2–4 heads — [TourBox chibi guide](https://www.tourboxtech.com/en/news/how-to-draw-chibi.html)
- Several guides state the "classic" chibi head-to-body ratio as 1:1 (head as tall as the rest of the body) or even head larger than body — [Hogonext](https://hogonext.com/?p=450294)
- Clip Studio has a dedicated guide on applying the same chibi rules to animals ("Animal and human chibis") — [Clip Studio Tips 4888](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/4888)
- Animal Crossing: in the first three games (GameCube, Wild World, City Folk) "a player character's head makes up half his height"; later 3D games moved human characters closer to 6–7 heads (fan style guide, not Nintendo) — [DX Town art style page](https://pineight.com/mw/page/DX_Town_art_style.xhtml)
- Animal Crossing villagers are described as having "big heads, big eyes, bright colors, Super-Deformed proportions" — [TV Tropes](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Characters/AnimalCrossingVillagerTypes)
- Nintendo's villager design process starts each villager "as a silhouette" (reported from a Nintendo presentation via Nintendo Soup) — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-villager-design/)
- Tsum Tsum: toys are "shaped like round rectangles so they can be stacked easily into a pyramid", "ovular with a face on one end", and characters are "simplified and drawn in a 'chibi' Japanese style, with large eyes and cute, small features"; name from Japanese *tsumu* (to stack) — [Mickey Blog](https://mickeyblog.com/?p=1756); [D23](https://d23.com/?p=43055)
- Pokémon/Pikachu: designer Atsuko Nishida built the prototype "using large pixels on a computer screen"; the original design "was not deemed quite 'cute enough'" so she switched to a squirrel reference for the round body and cheek pouches; Pikachu "has a round body with a larger back end and often sits on its haunches"; Ken Sugimori "felt that the game needed more 'cute' designs" and sought input from female staff; the "mouse" label was applied afterwards by Satoshi Tajiri — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/pikachu-originally-second-evolution-called-gorochu-large-fangs-two-horns/); [Star-Advertiser / Yomiuri](https://www.staradvertiser.com/2018/05/17/news/pikachu-inspired-by-a-squirrel-creators-reveal/); [Wikipedia: Pikachu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pikachu)
- One account lists Pikachu's design criteria as "a warm design, a non-threatening color, a face that could convey emotion, a vocalization pronounceable by children, and a cute appearance" (attribution unclear) — [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/stories/310443-game-freak-goes-into-great-detail-on-the-creation-of-pikachu)
- Sanrio style is described in general terms as "large eyes, small features, and soft colors" and "rounded shapes, pastel colors, and expressive eyes" (secondary blog sources only) — [ZenPop: what makes a drawing kawaii](https://zenpop.jp/blog/post/5213/what-makes-a-drawing-kawaii)

### Inferences
- For a 20–30 voxel tall quadruped, use a 2 to 2.5 "head" scheme: head block 10–12 voxels tall on a 24-voxel figure (about 45–50% of height), body about the same height or slightly less, legs 3–4 voxels. At 30 voxels: head 12–14, body 10–12, legs 4–5. This matches the "half the height is head" ratio of early Animal Crossing and the 2-head super-chibi standard.
- For quadrupeds the head should also be at least as wide as the body, or the head reads as "small"; chibi width rules are rarely stated, but Tsum Tsum (whole character is essentially one rounded block) shows you can collapse body into head entirely and still read as the character provided the ears/markings survive.
- Pikachu's case shows pixel-first design is a legitimate path to a cute mascot: the constraints of "large pixels" pushed the design toward round mass and a few strong identifiers (ears, cheeks, tail).

### Gaps
- No official Nintendo, Sanrio, Pokémon or Tsum Tsum style guide with numeric head-to-body ratios was found; a page claiming "Nintendo published official head-to-body ratios" was on a spam domain and is unverified.
- Animal Crossing villager (quadruped-origin animal) proportions were not measured by any source found; the half-head figure is for the human player character in the first three games.

## Key question 3: How should eyes be sized, spaced and placed, and does a catchlight matter?

### Takeaway
Put the eyes on or below the horizontal midline of the head (kawaii guides push them to the lower third), make them large with a big dark pupil/iris, space them wide, and give each a matching highlight; eye size alone measurably raises cuteness, and a highlight is what makes an eye read as "alive" rather than "dead".

### Cited Findings
- Kawaii guidance: "the eyes must sit on or below the horizontal midline of the face. This creates a vast expanse of 'forehead' space" that reads as vulnerable and non-threatening; "In most human faces, the eyes sit at the vertical midpoint of the head. In this style, we shove them down to the lower third or even the bottom quarter." — [ZenPop: what makes a drawing kawaii](https://zenpop.jp/blog/post/5213/what-makes-a-drawing-kawaii)
- Eye size alone affects cuteness: enlarged eyes rated cuter than original and reduced eyes across expressions and ages (Yao et al. 2022) — [Frontiers PDF](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.674456/pdf)
- Viewers look longer at the eye region of high-infantile faces; the eyes, not nose/mouth, attract gaze (Borgi et al. 2014) — [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140721100119.htm)
- A larger iris relative to the eye reads as cuter and more innocent; highlight placement changes where the character appears to look; highlights must match across both eyes to avoid a cross-eyed look — [Autodesk community tutorial: cute eyes](https://origin-exchange.autodesk.com/how-to-draw-cute-eyes)
- A large pupil "is generally designed to feel warmer, as long as it isn't excessive"; highlight position and size shift the overall impression — [Clip Studio Tips: cartoon eyes](https://tips.clip-studio.com/en-us/articles/2614)
- The catchlight is what "gives eyes life and dimension"; an eye without one looks "dead" — [Photofocus: catchlights](https://photofocus.com/photography/understanding-the-magic-of-catchlights/); [DIY Photography: Catchlights 101](https://www.diyphotography.net/?p=142990)
- In chibi styles highlights are "big and bold" with simple irises to maximise cuteness — [How to draw cute eyes (PDF mirror)](https://wiki.uep.edu.py/default.aspx/TCfemt/706780/How To Draw Cute Eyes.pdf)
- Tsum Tsum and Sanrio: "large eyes and cute, small features" — [Mickey Blog](https://mickeyblog.com/?p=1756)

### Inferences
- Voxel translation for a head block H voxels tall: place the eye row's centre at 55–65% of the way down from the top of the head (i.e. in the lower half), leaving at least 40% of head height as "forehead" above the eyes. On a 10-voxel head: eyes occupying rows 6–7 from the top.
- Eye size: for a 10–12 voxel head, a 2x2 or 2x3 voxel eye is "big"; 1x1 reads as a bead and loses the baby-schema effect. Keep eye height greater than or equal to the nose/mouth combined.
- Spacing: at least one eye-width (2–3 voxels) between the eyes; kawaii faces are wide-set because the face is wide and flat (quadruped heads viewed from the front are naturally wide, which helps).
- Catchlight in voxels: a single light voxel (white or very pale) in the upper-left (or upper-outer) corner of each 2x2 dark eye, identical position on both eyes. On a 3x3 eye, a 1-voxel highlight plus a coloured iris ring is possible. A 2x2 eye with 1 highlight voxel is 25% highlight, which is in the "big and bold chibi" range.
- Keep the pupil/iris dark and large; the sclera (white) can be dropped entirely (Sanrio/Animal Crossing style "dot eyes") on very small heads.

### Gaps
- No experimental study gives eye size as a percentage of head height for maximum cuteness; the "lower third" rule is from practitioner guides, not research.
- No research on catchlights and cuteness specifically; the "alive vs dead" claim comes from photography/illustration practice.
- No source gives an inter-ocular distance rule; the spacing figures above are inference.

## Key question 4: What motion and behaviour cues read as cute (hop, wiggle, head tilt, blink timing, squash-and-stretch)?

### Takeaway
Cute motion is small, bouncy, slightly unstable and "unprompted": idle blinks every ~2–4 s, head tilts on noticing, little hops, and squash-and-stretch on landings with volume preserved; Japanese mascot (yuru-chara) convention explicitly prizes "unstable or awkward" movement, and research shows behaviour changes cuteness ratings independent of appearance.

### Cited Findings
- Idle animations exist "to give personality towards games and their characters" since they are among the few moments a character acts without input — [Wikipedia: Idle animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idle_animation)
- Blinks and facial expressions are secondary actions that "make characters even more alive"; appeal "can also apply to the way they move" — [cxong: 12 principles](https://cxong.github.io/2023/03/12-principles-of-animation)
- One developer uses "a blink every 2.5 seconds as an overlay on the protagonist's idle animation"; another times the blink to the frame where the head nods — [GDevelop forum thread](https://forum.gdevelop.io/t/solved-making-my-character-blink/46268)
- Players ask for "a small hop" and "a cute head tilt when they notice something" for a pixel companion; another praises "the little hopping animations of the characters, and they looks so cute!" — [r/PixelArt thread](https://lr.in.psf.lt/r/PixelArt/comments/1jzr434/little_companion_i_need_to_finish); [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/5706912)
- Feedback on a shopkeeper idle: "Very subtle but still bouncy and fun"; suggestion to add "a slight bend at the knees" for lower-body movement — [Roblox devforum](https://devforum.roblox.com/t/idle-animation-feedback/3484833)
- Subtle blinks can go unnoticed consciously while still adding life ("i didnt even realise that some characters blinked") — [itch.io comment](https://itch.io/post/14566909)
- Squash and stretch was called "by far the most important" principle by Thomas & Johnston; Walt Stanchfield's view: a simple shape plus squash and stretch is enough anatomy for cartoon characters — [Wikipedia: Squash and stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_and_stretch)
- Keep volume constant: squash flattens under pressure, stretch adds a sense of speed; stronger squash/stretch looks smoother and bouncier "as long as volume is maintained" — [UW CSE 557 lecture](https://courses.cs.washington.edu/courses/cse557/06wi/lectures/animation.pdf); [BINUS DKV](https://binus.ac.id/bandung/dkv/wp-json/wp/v2/posts/957)
- Appeal is not the same as cuteness: "all characters need appeal, whether heroic, evil, comic, or cute" — [WKU animation principles](https://people.wku.edu/joon.sung/edu/anim/3d/theory/principles.html)
- Yuru-chara informal rules: movements should be "unique, unstable or awkward"; designs should be "absolutely not complex but simple and functional"; kawaii attributes include "innocence (childish or unpredictable postures)" and "sweetness (soft or bright details)" — [Wikipedia: Yuru-kyara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuru-kyara); [Eroica Fenice](https://www.eroicafenice.com/salotto-culturale/mascotte-giapponesi-cosa-sono-e-perche-sono-popolari); [Thai JICA study](https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jica/article/download/244123/166764/868006)
- Behaviour/personality beliefs alter perceived cuteness independently of static features (Koyama et al. 2006; Karraker & Stern 1990) — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)
- Cute targets interacting affectionately evoke more kama muta (Steinnes et al. 2019) — [Frontiers 2019](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.00387/pdf)
- Auditory cuteness: high-frequency, pure-tone-like infant sounds attract caregivers across species (Kringelbach et al. 2016) — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4956347)

### Inferences
- Voxel idle recipe: (a) blink: swap the 2x2 eye voxels for a 1-voxel-high dark line for 2–3 frames (~100–150 ms) every 2.5–4 s with random jitter, both eyes simultaneously; (b) breathing/bounce: body block moves 1 voxel up/down on a ~1.5–2 s cycle (a slow 1-voxel bob reads as breathing); (c) head tilt: rotate or offset the head block 1 voxel to one side when the player approaches or an item appears; (d) hop: whole character rises 2–3 voxels with the body squashed 1 voxel shorter and 1 voxel wider on takeoff and landing (preserve volume: shorter must mean wider); (e) wiggle: ear/tail block flips 1 voxel every few seconds.
- Squash-and-stretch limits for blocks: because curves are impossible, scale whole blocks non-uniformly in 1-voxel increments (e.g. 10 tall x 8 wide -> 9 x 9 on squash). More than ~20% deformation on a blocky model starts to break the "solid toy" read; keep within 1–2 voxels.
- The yuru-chara "unstable/awkward" rule suggests a slightly off-balance landing (one extra wobble frame) is cuter than a clean mechanical landing.
- Pair motion with a short high-pitched chirp/squeak sound; Kringelbach's auditory cuteness finding supports this.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study on which specific animations (hop vs tilt vs wiggle) maximise rated cuteness; the evidence is practitioner consensus and player comments.
- Blink timing (2.5 s) is one developer's choice; no source gives a validated "cute" blink rate.
- No published squash-and-stretch percentage thresholds for cute characters were found.

## Key question 5: How do you keep an animal recognisable while babyfying it (which species features to exaggerate rather than remove)?

### Takeaway
Simplification guides converge on silhouette-first design: strip detail but keep or enlarge the one or two "signature" features that define the species (ears, snout, horns, comb, tail), and preserve the real animal's basic underlying forms; babyfying should shrink the snout/jaw and limbs but never the identifying appendages, and should colour-code the species with its iconic palette.

### Cited Findings
- A character "should have a distinct outline; that outline keeps it readable in complex scenes"; designers thumbnail many silhouettes and pick the strongest — [Fiveable: character silhouettes](https://fiveable.me/2d-animation/unit-13/creating-character-silhouettes-shapes/study-guide/1oo2tSb8S1byAuIV); [ArtWod: stylised body shapes](https://artwod.com/blog/stylised-body-shapes-simplify-and-exaggerate-figures-for-animation-and-illustration)
- "Exaggerated features help viewers pick out the character's key qualities"; animal-specific targets are "large ears, a long tail, or a wide grin" and oversized eyes — [Nalena: cartoon characteristics](https://nalena.com/cartoon-characteristic/); [ArtWod](https://artwod.com/blog/stylised-body-shapes-simplify-and-exaggerate-figures-for-animation-and-illustration)
- "Simplify to an iconic symbol by exaggerating a significant feature or using the full animal's shape" — [Fiveable](https://fiveable.me/2d-animation/unit-13/creating-character-silhouettes-shapes/study-guide/1oo2tSb8S1byAuIV)
- "Even while simplifying, basic animal forms must be understood"; cartoon animals reduce to fundamental shapes — [WKU: what makes a good character](https://people.wku.edu/joon.sung/edu/anim/3d/theory/good_character.html)
- Nintendo starts each Animal Crossing villager as a silhouette — [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/animal-crossing-new-horizons-villager-design/)
- Pikachu retained its species signifiers (ears, cheek pouches, tail) while the body was rounded for cuteness; the "mouse" classification was added later, i.e. the design reads as a generic cute rodent, not a specific species — [Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/pikachu-originally-second-evolution-called-gorochu-large-fangs-two-horns/)
- Yuru-chara must incorporate "motifs that represent local culture, history or produce" and "it can be hard to make it as a yuru-chara with only an adorable design. Recognizable, memorable features matter too" — [Wikipedia: Yuru-kyara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuru-kyara); [Tokyo Weekender](https://www.tokyoweekender.com/art_and_culture/history/japanese-mascots/)
- Teddy bears were babyfied by shortening the snout and limbs, but remain bears because ears, fur colour and overall body form were kept — [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bear-culture-nature-heritage/out-of-the-wild-wood-and-into-our-beds-the-evolutionary-history-of-teddy-bears-and-the-natural-selection-of-deadly-cuteness/658587402AE79C17433CB3D9FA9E8C55)
- Baby schema has an optimum: beyond a certain degree, cuteness ratings fall (Kiss-Leizer 2017, 0–60% application) — [MTAK](https://real.mtak.hu/60111/)
- Dog breeding toward brachycephaly (French Bulldog) shows babyfied snouts in real animals still read as "dog" because other breed cues remain — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness)

### Inferences
- Rule: babyfy the *baby-schema* features (head size, forehead, eyes, snout length, limb length), but never the *identity* features. For farm animals the identity features are, per species: cow = wide ears sticking out sideways, small horn nubs, pink muzzle patch, black-and-white patches, udder (optional); pig = big round snout disc with two nostrils (keep it LARGE and pink, flatten it onto the face rather than extending it), floppy triangular ears, curly tail; sheep = cloud-shaped wool mass, dark face/legs, small ears sticking out; chicken = red comb and wattle, yellow beak, tail fan, orange legs; goat = horns, beard, slit eyes (or keep round eyes and signal "goat" with beard + horns); duck = flat wide bill, tail curl; horse = mane, long ears, muzzle. These are all silhouette or high-contrast colour features that survive at 1–3 voxels.
- In voxels, "shrink the snout" means make it flat (1 voxel protrusion) but keep its *width* and colour (pig snout 3–4 voxels wide, 1 deep; cow muzzle 5–6 voxels wide, 1 deep). A flat, wide, pink nose patch preserves identity while meeting the "small nose" baby cue because it no longer projects.
- Colour is a free identity channel on a blocky model: Holstein patches, pink pig, white wool, red comb carry species recognition without extra geometry, so geometry can be spent on baby-schema.
- Stop-point for babyfying: when the identity features drop below ~2 voxels or when head width exceeds the ear span (ears no longer stick out past the head), the species reads as a generic blob. Test by rendering the character as a flat black silhouette; if it is not identifiable in silhouette plus one flat colour, restore an identity feature before adding cuteness.
- Consistency matters: keep the same head-to-body ratio, eye size and eye row across all species so they read as one set (as Tsum Tsum does).

### Gaps
- No source gives a measured threshold for how far a specific species (cow, pig) can be babyfied before recognition fails; this is practitioner inference plus the general inverted-U evidence.
- Official Nintendo or Sanrio notes on retaining species features were not found.

## Key question 6 (from objective): Rounded vs sharp forms, limb stubbiness, and colour choices

### Takeaway
Round, soft, simple forms are a consistent descriptor of kawaii/chibi/mascot style and match the "rounder face" factor in the baby-schema experiments; colour guidance is practitioner-level only: soft pastels or desaturated mid-tones with warm accents, non-threatening hues, and consistent palette use across the character set.

### Cited Findings
- "Rounder face" is one of the four manipulated baby-schema features in Glocker 2009 and Borgi 2014; narrow faces lowered cuteness — [Penn repository](https://repository.upenn.edu/cog_neuro_pubs/11); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2014/07/140721100119.htm)
- Sanrio/kawaii described as "rounded shapes, pastel colors, and expressive eyes" and "large eyes, small features, and soft colors" — [ZenPop](https://zenpop.jp/blog/post/5213/what-makes-a-drawing-kawaii)
- Kawaii palette references: "soft pastels such as pink, mint, and lavender, with a light pink or cream background"; pastels are "pale, light, and desaturated" — [Icons8 pastel palette article](https://blog.icons8.com/articles/pastel-color-palette/)
- "The consistent use of the chosen palette across all character representations reinforces the cute aesthetic" — [Nalena](https://nalena.com/cartoon-characteristic/)
- Pikachu criteria included "a warm design, a non-threatening color" — [GoNintendo](https://gonintendo.com/stories/310443-game-freak-goes-into-great-detail-on-the-creation-of-pikachu)
- Yuru-chara "sweetness" attribute: "soft or bright details" — [Thai JICA study](https://so02.tci-thaijo.org/index.php/jica/article/download/244123/166764/868006)
- A simple shape plus squash and stretch is sufficient anatomy for cartoon characters (Stanchfield) — [Wikipedia: Squash and stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_and_stretch)
- Lorenz's list (as paraphrased in the literature) includes a plump, rounded body and short, thick extremities; Hinde & Barden document teddy bears moving from "long-limbed" to short-limbed baby-like proportions — [Wikipedia: Cuteness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuteness); [Cambridge chapter](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/bear-culture-nature-heritage/out-of-the-wild-wood-and-into-our-beds-the-evolutionary-history-of-teddy-bears-and-the-natural-selection-of-deadly-cuteness/658587402AE79C17433CB3D9FA9E8C55)

### Inferences
- "Round" in voxels: avoid 1-voxel-wide protrusions and long thin elements; chamfer the head block's corners (remove the 8 corner voxels, or the corner columns on a 10+ voxel head) so the silhouette reads as a rounded square; make the body a cube-ish block rather than a long box (length no more than 1.3x height); make ears and tails 2 voxels thick rather than 1.
- Limb stubbiness in voxels: legs 2–3 wide and 3–5 tall on a 24–30 voxel character (legs about 15–20% of height), no visible knees, feet same width as legs or 1 wider ("socks" or hooves as a single contrasting voxel row).
- Colour: use desaturated mid-to-light values (roughly 20–50% saturation, 70–90% lightness for body colours), one warm accent (pink muzzle, red comb, peach cheeks) and a single darker outline-ish tone for eyes; add 2x1 pink "blush" voxels under the eyes for the "round cheeks" cue, which doubles as warmth. Avoid pure black (use very dark brown/navy for eyes) and avoid fully saturated primaries except on identity features (chicken comb).
- Lighting: a slightly lighter top face on each block (voxel ambient occlusion / top-light) gives the "soft highlight" look without any extra geometry.

### Gaps
- No experimental research was found linking specific hues or saturation levels to perceived cuteness; colour guidance here is from design practice and palette collections, not studies.
- No source gives numeric saturation/lightness ranges for "cute" palettes; the percentages above are inference.
